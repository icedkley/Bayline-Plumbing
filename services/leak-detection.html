Signs you have a leak

None of these on their own prove a leak, but two or more usually means water is escaping somewhere.

Water bill jumped unexpectedly

A bill that's 20%+ higher than usual with no change in habits usually means water is escaping somewhere.

Water stains on walls or ceilings

Brown or yellow rings, bubbling paint, or soft drywall — water is pooling behind the surface.

Musty smell in a room

Persistent damp odor in a basement, bathroom, or under a sink — usually hidden moisture.

Sound of running water when nothing is on

A faint hiss or trickle that never stops — often a supply line or toilet flapper.

Warm spot on the floor

With a slab leak, hot water lines under concrete can warm the floor above them.

Cracks in the foundation or tile

Ongoing slab leaks can shift soil under the foundation and crack tile or drywall.

How we find and fix it

Two steps: pinpoint the leak without tearing things open, then repair the actual cause.

Find it

We start non-invasively. Acoustic listening equipment, moisture meters, and thermal imaging narrow down where the leak is before anything gets opened up. For slab leaks, we use pressure testing on the hot and cold lines to tell which side of the system is losing water.

Get an estimate

Fix it

The repair depends on what's leaking and where. A dripping fixture gets a new cartridge or valve. A pinhole leak in an accessible pipe gets a repair or a short repipe. A slab leak may need rerouting above the slab instead of breaking concrete. We quote the repair before we start.

What it costs

Leak detection is a diagnostic service — we charge for finding the leak, not for guessing at it. The fee covers the equipment and time it takes to pinpoint the source. If you approve the repair, that detection work informs the quote.

Estimates for the repair are free. The detection fee is quoted upfront. If we can't find the leak, we'll tell you that too — you won't be charged for guesswork.

Leak detection typically runs $250–$500 depending on complexity. Repairs are quoted separately once we know what we're dealing with.

Leak detection questions

Straight answers to what people ask before booking a detection visit.

Acoustic listening equipment picks up the sound of water escaping under pressure. Moisture meters tell us where the drywall or slab is wet. Thermal imaging shows temperature differences from water. Between those three, we can usually pinpoint the leak within a foot or two before we open anything up.

A slab leak is a leak in a water line running under the concrete foundation of your home. They're common in older homes with copper supply lines. The symptom is often a warm spot on the floor or a water bill that keeps climbing with no visible source.
