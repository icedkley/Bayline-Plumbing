What counts as an emergency

If it involves water or sewage where it shouldn't be, it's worth a call. Here's what we treat as urgent.

Burst pipe or active leak

Water coming through a wall, ceiling, or floor — shut off the main and call.

No water at all

Every tap is dry — often a main line issue, pressure problem, or frozen section.

Sewage backing up

Waste water rising in a tub, shower, or floor drain — a blocked main line, not a sink clog.

Water heater leaking

Any pooling around the base of the tank — the sooner it's looked at, the less damage.

Toilet overflowing and won't stop

The flapper or fill valve has failed. Shut off the supply valve behind the toilet and call.

Gas smell near a water heater

Leave the area, don't flip switches, and call your gas utility first — then call us.

How we handle an emergency call

Two priorities: stop the damage, then fix the cause. In that order.

Stop the problem

The first priority is stopping water or sewage from causing more damage. We'll walk you through shutting off the right valve if you haven't already, then get someone heading your way. For active leaks and backups, we bring the tools to isolate the problem on the first visit.

Call now

Repair it

Once the immediate issue is under control, we diagnose and repair the cause — the failed pipe, the blocked main, the leaking tank, the broken valve. You'll get a straight explanation of what failed, what it takes to fix it, and what it costs before we start.

What it costs

Emergency work is priced the same way as any other job: by the problem, not by the hour. We'll tell you what we can before we arrive, and confirm once we've seen it.

Estimates are free. If it's a repair we can do on the spot, we'll quote it before we start. If it's something bigger — a main line, a repipe, a full replacement — we'll give you the number and the next step.

Most emergency repairs run $150–$500. Main line and sewer emergencies vary more — we'll quote before we start.
