Signs you need drain cleaning

Any one of these on its own is a sign. Two or more usually means the blockage is bigger than a single fixture.

Water drains slowly

Sinks, tubs, or showers that pool and empty gradually — a partial clog building up.

Standing water in a sink or tub

Water that won't drain at all — the blockage has closed the line.

Recurring clogs in the same drain

If the same drain clogs every few months, the blockage isn't fully cleared.

Gurgling or bubbling sounds

Air escaping through a partial blockage — often a sign the issue is deeper in the line.

Foul smell from the drain

Odor rising from a sink, tub, or floor drain — buildup or a dry trap.

Multiple drains backed up at once

More than one drain slow or backed up usually means a main line blockage, not a single fixture.

How we clear it

Two questions: where is the blockage, and why did it form? The answer determines the method.

Clear the blockage

We start by finding out where the clog actually is — a single fixture, a branch line, or the main. Most kitchen and bath clogs clear with a hand auger or a drain machine. Main line blockages get a larger cutter head and a camera to confirm the line is clear and sound.

Keep it from coming back

If the same drain keeps clogging, there's usually an underlying cause — grease buildup, tree roots in the line, or a bellied pipe. We'll tell you what we found and whether a simple cleaning will hold or whether the line needs a closer look.

What it costs

Drain cleaning is priced by the type of blockage and where it sits in the system. A single kitchen sink is a different job than a main line clear-out, and we quote it that way.

Estimates are free. If we can clear it on the spot, we'll quote before we start. If the problem turns out to be bigger — roots, a collapsed section, or a recurring main line issue — we'll tell you and give you the next step.

Most drain cleanings run $150–$400. Main line clean-outs and camera inspections run more — we'll quote before we start.
